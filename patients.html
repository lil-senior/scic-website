Cardiac MRI

What is cardiac MRI?

Cardiac MRI (also called CMR) uses a powerful magnet and radio waves to create detailed images of your heart. Unlike X-rays or CT scans, there is no radiation involved. It is one of the safest and most detailed ways to look at the heart.

Your doctor has referred you for a cardiac MRI because it provides information that other tests cannot. CMR can show:

✓ How well your heart is pumping and how the chambers are moving
✓ Whether there is scarring, inflammation, or damage to the heart muscle
✓ How blood flows through the heart and major blood vessels
✓ The structure of heart valves and surrounding tissue

Why your doctor chose CMR

❤
Most detailed heart images available

CMR is considered the gold standard for assessing heart structure and function

⛺
No radiation exposure

Safe for repeated scans, making it ideal for monitoring your condition over time

🔍
Sees what other tests cannot

Tissue characterization reveals inflammation, scarring, and other changes invisible to ultrasound

Your Visit

What to expect during your cardiac MRI

A cardiac MRI typically takes 45 to 75 minutes. Here is a step-by-step guide so you know exactly what will happen.

Before your scan

Arrival & Safety Screening

Arrive 30 minutes early. You will be asked to change into a hospital gown (if needed) and complete a safety questionnaire. Please tell the team about any implants, metal in your body, or devices (pacemakers, joint replacements, etc.).

Getting ready

Positioning & ECG Leads

You will lie on a padded table. Small sensor pads (ECG leads) are placed on your chest so the scanner can synchronize with your heartbeat. You will receive headphones, the scanner is loud but not painful.

During the scan

Inside the Scanner

The table slides into a wide, well-lit tunnel. You will hear knocking and buzzing sounds — this is normal. The technologist will talk to you throughout and may ask you to hold your breath for short periods (10-15 seconds) to get the best images.

Contrast (if needed)

Gadolinium Injection

Many cardiac MRI scans include a contrast agent called gadolinium, given through a small IV in your arm. This helps highlight areas of the heart muscle and is very safe. Some scans may also include adenosine, which briefly increases blood flow to test for blockages.

After the scan

All Done

Results are sent to your referring doctor.

How to prepare

✓ You may eat and drink normally before the scan (unless told otherwise)
✓ Take your regular medications as usual
✓ Leave jewellery, watches, and metal accessories at home
✓ Bring your Alberta Health Care card and identification. If available, please bring your referral paperwork as well.
✓ Arrive 30 minutes before your appointment
Implants & Devices

If you have a pacemaker, defibrillator (ICD), cochlear implant, or any metal implants, please inform us when booking. Many modern devices are MRI-compatible, but we need to verify this before your scan.

Q&A

Frequently asked questions

Is cardiac MRI safe?

+

Yes. Cardiac MRI uses a magnetic field and radio waves, there is no ionizing radiation. It is one of the safest imaging techniques available and has been used in millions of patients worldwide.

Will it hurt?

+

No. The scan itself is completely painless. If contrast is needed, you will feel a small needle prick for the IV, and possibly a brief cool sensation when the contrast is injected. If adenosine stress testing is performed, you may feel a temporary warm flush, mild chest tightness, or shortness of breath. These sensations are expected and resolve within a minute.

How long does the scan take?

+

Most cardiac MRI scans take between 45 and 75 minutes. Some complex studies or stress tests may take up to 90 minutes. Plan to be at the centre for about 1.5 to 2 hours in total, including preparation time.

What if I am claustrophobic?

+

Most patients manage well. If you are anxious, speak to your referring doctor beforehand as a mild sedative can be prescribed. However, all our technologists are experienced in helping patients stay calm and comfortable.

Can I have an MRI with a pacemaker or ICD?

+

In many cases, yes. Most modern pacemakers and defibrillators are labelled "MR-conditional," meaning they are safe for MRI under specific conditions. Our team will verify your device before scheduling your scan. If you have an older or non-conditional device, we will discuss your options with you.

What is gadolinium contrast?

+

Gadolinium is a contrast agent given through a vein to improve the detail of MRI images. It highlights areas of scarring, inflammation, or abnormal blood flow in the heart. It is generally very safe. If you have kidney problems, please let us know, as the dose may need to be adjusted.

Why do I have to hold my breath?

+

Breathing causes your chest to move, which blurs the images. Short breath-holds (10-15 seconds) keep the heart still so we can capture sharp, detailed pictures. The technologist will guide you through each breath-hold. If you have difficulty, we can adapt the scan with free-breathing techniques.

When will I get my results?

+

Your cardiac MRI images are interpreted by a specialist. Results are sent to your referring doctor. Your doctor will discuss the results with you at your next appointment.

Do I need to do anything after the scan?

+

No special care is needed. You can eat, drink, drive, and return to all normal activities immediately after the scan. If you received gadolinium contrast, drinking extra water will help your body clear it more quickly.
