Cardiac MRI

What is cardiac MRI?

Cardiac magnetic resonance imaging, or CMR, uses a magnetic field and radio waves to create detailed images of the heart. It does not use ionizing radiation.

CMR can provide information about heart size and function, blood flow, heart muscle injury or scarring, and the structure of the heart and major blood vessels.

Your Visit

What to expect during your cardiac MRI

A cardiac MRI typically takes 45 to 75 minutes. Here is a step-by-step guide so you know exactly what will happen.

Before your scan

Arrival & Safety Screening

You will complete an MRI safety screening and may be asked to change into a hospital gown. Please tell the team about any implanted devices, metal in your body or previous procedures.

Getting ready

Positioning & ECG Leads

You will lie on a padded table. Small sensor pads (ECG leads) are placed on your chest so the scanner can synchronize with your heartbeat. An intravenous line may be placed if contrast or medications are required.

During the scan

Inside the Scanner

You will lie on a table that moves into the MRI scanner. The scanner makes loud knocking and buzzing sounds, and you will be given hearing protection. The technologist can communicate with you throughout the examination. You may be asked to hold your breath briefly for some images.

Contrast (if needed)

Gadolinium Injection

Some examinations use gadolinium contrast. Some patients also undergo stress perfusion imaging using a medication that temporarily increases blood flow to the heart. Your team will explain these parts of the examination if they are needed.

After the scan

All Done

Your images will be reviewed by a cardiac imaging physician, and the report will be sent to your referring healthcare provider.

How to prepare

✓ Please follow the preparation instructions provided when your appointment is booked. Instructions may differ depending on the type of cardiac MRI you are having.
✓ Leave jewellery, watches, and removable metal items at home when possible.
✓ Bring your Alberta Health Care card and identification. If available, please bring your referral paperwork as well.
✓ Bring information about any pacemaker, defibrillator, implanted device or previous surgery if available.
✓ Arrive at the time provided in your appointment instructions.
Implants & Devices

Having an implanted device does not necessarily mean that you cannot have an MRI. However, all devices and implants must be reviewed for MRI safety before your examination. Please tell the booking team if you have a pacemaker, defibrillator, cochlear implant or other implanted medical device.

Having a stress cardiac MRI?

Stress cardiac MRI requires additional preparation, including restrictions on caffeine before your examination. Please follow the instructions provided when your appointment is booked. Contact the CMR team if you are unsure.

Q&A

Frequently asked questions

Is cardiac MRI safe?

+

Yes. Cardiac MRI uses a magnetic field and radio waves, there is no ionizing radiation. It is one of the safest imaging techniques available and has been used in millions of patients worldwide.

Will the scan hurt?

+

No. The MRI scan itself is painless. You may feel a brief needle prick if an IV is required. If you are having a stress CMR, the stress medication can cause temporary symptoms such as flushing, shortness of breath or chest discomfort. The team will monitor you throughout the examination.

How long does the scan take?

+

Most cardiac MRI scans take between 45 and 75 minutes. Some complex studies or stress tests may take up to 90 minutes. Plan to be at the centre for about 1.5 to 2 hours in total, including preparation time.

What if I am claustrophobic?

+

Please let your referring physician or the CMR team know before your appointment if you are concerned about claustrophobia. We can discuss ways to help you complete the examination comfortably.

Can I have an MRI with a pacemaker or ICD?

+

In many cases, yes. Most modern pacemakers and defibrillators are labelled "MR-conditional," meaning they are safe for MRI under specific conditions. Our team will verify your device before scheduling your scan. If you have an older or non-conditional device, we will discuss your options with you.

When will I receive my results?

+

A cardiac imaging physician will review your study and send the report to your referring healthcare provider, who will discuss the results with you.
