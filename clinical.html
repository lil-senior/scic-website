Cardiovascular Disease and CMR

Cardiovascular disease is the leading cause of death in the developed world, with one Canadian dying every seven minutes. In an effort to combat this, Canada spends $21 billion every year on cardiovascular therapy. Still, there are over 500,000 Canadians living with heart failure and 40,000 Canadians succumbing to sudden cardiac death each year. In North America, 1000 people die every day from sudden cardiac death, establishing it as the leading cause of death in people over 40 years of age.

Since its introduction, cardiovascular MRI has become one of the most advanced and versatile tools for evaluating heart health. It provides unparalleled insight into cardiac structure, function, blood flow, and tissue characteristics such as inflammation and scarring.

Why Cardiac MRI?

The gold standard for cardiac imaging

Cardiac MRI offers unmatched tissue characterization, accurate volumetric assessment, and comprehensive functional evaluation — all without radiation exposure.

❤
Myocardial Tissue Characterization

T1 mapping, T2 mapping, and late gadolinium enhancement for fibrosis, oedema, and inflammation

📊
Precise Volumetric Assessment

Gold-standard LV/RV volumes, mass, and ejection fraction without geometric assumptions

🔍
No Ionizing Radiation

Safe for repeated assessment, particularly important for younger patients and serial monitoring

Clinical Indications

What to refer for cardiac MRI

Expand each category to see specific indications and what cardiac MRI adds to clinical management.

Cardiomyopathies

+
Hypertrophic Cardiomyopathy (HCM)

Pattern of hypertrophy, LGE for fibrosis burden, SCD risk stratification

Dilated Cardiomyopathy (DCM)

Differentiate ischemic vs non-ischemic, LGE pattern for prognosis

Arrhythmogenic Cardiomyopathy (ARVC)

RV morphology, fat infiltration, wall motion abnormalities

Cardiac Amyloidosis

Diffuse LGE, native T1 elevation, ECV quantification

Protocol note: Most cardiomyopathy assessments include cine imaging, T1/T2 mapping, and late gadolinium enhancement. Typical scan time: 60 minutes.

Myocarditis & Inflammatory Heart Disease

+
Acute Myocarditis

Lake Louise criteria: T2 oedema, T1/ECV elevation, epicardial LGE

Post-Vaccine Myocarditis

Characterization and monitoring of inflammatory injury

Cardiac Sarcoidosis

Patchy septal LGE, granulomatous involvement pattern

Pericardial Disease

Pericardial thickness, effusion, constriction physiology

Ischemic Heart Disease & Viability

+
Myocardial Viability

Transmurality of LGE to guide revascularization decisions

Stress Perfusion CMR

Adenosine or regadenoson stress for ischemia detection; high sensitivity & specificity

Post-MI Assessment

Infarct size, microvascular obstruction, LV remodelling

Congenital Heart Disease

+

Comprehensive assessment of complex congenital anatomy, RV/LV volumes, valvular regurgitation, and shunt quantification. 4D flow capability for haemodynamic assessment.

Valvular Heart Disease

+

Phase-contrast flow imaging for regurgitant volumes, planimetry of stenotic valves, and ventricular adaptation assessment for timing of intervention.

Cardiac Masses & Oncology

+

Tissue characterization of intracardiac masses (thrombus vs tumour), cardio-oncology surveillance for chemotherapy-related cardiomyopathy, and pre-operative planning.

Professional Education

Education for Cardiac Imaging Professionals

Beyond clinical service, SCIC is committed to advancing the knowledge and skills of cardiac imaging professionals through courses, events, and continuing education.

Annual CMR Course

A comprehensive yearly course covering the fundamentals and advanced applications of cardiac MRI. Features lectures, hands-on workshops, and case-based learning from SCIC faculty and international guest speakers.

Next course: Date to be announced

Meet & Greet

An informal networking event held alongside our annual course, bringing together referring cardiologists, imaging specialists, researchers, and industry partners. A chance to build connections and discuss the future of cardiac imaging.

Readers Day

An intensive one-day session focused on CMR image interpretation. Participants work through a curated set of clinical cases under expert guidance — from straightforward to complex. Ideal for cardiologists building their reading skills.
