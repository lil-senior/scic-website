The Vision

What is interventional CMR?

Interventional cardiac MRI (iCMR) uses real-time MRI to guide catheters and devices inside the heart — replacing X-ray fluoroscopy with radiation-free, soft-tissue imaging. This means cardiologists can see the heart muscle, valves, and scar tissue while performing procedures.

iCMR has the potential to transform how we treat arrhythmias, structural heart disease, and congenital heart conditions — offering safer procedures with better outcomes.

iCMR Research

Our translational research programme

From bench to bedside — SCIC is building one of North America's leading iCMR research programmes, spanning device development, imaging innovation, and clinical translation.

MRI-Guided Catheterization

Developing real-time MRI pulse sequences optimized for catheter tracking and visualization. Working with MRI-compatible catheter systems to enable diagnostic and therapeutic procedures entirely under MRI guidance.

Real-time imaging Catheter tracking Device safety

Ablation Lesion Visualization

Developing gadolinium-free MRI methods to visualize radiofrequency ablation lesions in real time. This allows immediate assessment of whether the ablation was successful — during the procedure itself, not days later.

Gadolinium-free Native T1/T2 Acute assessment

ECG Integration in MRI

12-lead MR-compatible ECG and electrocardiographic imaging (ECGi) to provide fused electro-anatomical maps. Combines electrical mapping with MRI tissue characterization for comprehensive arrhythmia substrate assessment.

12-lead ECG ECGi MHD correction

Pre-Clinical Validation

Rigorous validation in phantom models and large animal studies before clinical translation. Building the safety and efficacy evidence required for regulatory approval of iCMR procedures in humans.
